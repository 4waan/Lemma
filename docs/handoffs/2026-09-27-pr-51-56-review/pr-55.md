### Findings

1. **Should fix before merge.** `PROVIDER_ADDRESS` is never compared with the address of `PROVIDER_PRIVATE_KEY` (`apps/server/src/config.ts:294-310`). Sales require every sellable release's `payTo` to equal `PROVIDER_ADDRESS` (`startup.ts:16-18`), `register-release` registers that address as the provider (`warranty/admin.ts:299`), and the activator requires the on-chain provider to equal the key's address (`warranty/actions.ts:356`). With a mismatch every activation is skipped as `RELEASE_ROLES_MISMATCH` at warn level while purchases with claims keep settling: buyers pay for warranties that never activate. One `ConfigError` in `warrantyConfig` plus a test.
2. **Should fix before merge (money, low likelihood).** A registry read that contradicts the indexed facts ends an action for good instead of backing off: `evaluator.ts:172` (`none` becomes `abandoned`), `expirer.ts:89` (`none` becomes `skipped`), `relay.ts:55` (anything but `failed` or `refunded` becomes `abandoned NO_CREDIT`). Each of these actions exists only because the indexer stored the matching event at least 64 blocks deep, so a correct chain can never answer `none` for them; a lagging backend of a load-balanced endpoint can, because `chain.ts` pins the reads to `getBlockNumber({ cacheTime: 0 })`, which such a node answers with an old number. A stale `none` on a finalization loses the buyer's credit (the warranty then expires); on an expiry it leaves bond reserved until someone calls `expireResolution` by hand; on a withdrawal it answers the bridge `abandoned` for good. Treat a status the action's origin rules out as a backoff (`STALE_READ`), keeping the `onRevert` paths as they are.
3. **Should fix before merge (operations).** `npm run evaluator -- list` prints how long a failed outcome has waited but not the claim deadline in force (`review.ts:77`), and nothing alerts before the window closes; the expirer expires an active warranty whose finalization is still in `review`, and `closeEndedReviews` then abandons it (`evaluator.ts:180`, `CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`). The contract forbids a late finalization anyway, so a slow operator silently turns a buyer's refund into the provider's bond. Print the deadline in `listReview` and log at error level when a review is within a few hours of it.
4. **Should fix soon (guards backlog item 20, decision D2, without a contract change).** `activator.ts:158` closes an activation as `done` whenever the on-chain status is not `none`, without comparing the on-chain `releaseDigest`, `profileIndex` and `amount` (all in `RegistryResolution`) with its own voucher. Another registered provider who activates the same resolution id first under its own release would make the server treat that as its own activation, finalize under the wrong release, and record the outcome against the wrong digest. Compare the three fields, and finish as `skipped` with an alert when they differ.
5. **Follow-up (item 46, confirmed with a probe).** `actions.ts:204-206` reads `nonces(sender)` and the registry outside the per-sender `serially()` queue in `chain.ts`, which wraps only the send. With the expirer sending from the provider account in the gap, a resend goes out with a nonce the other job used: refused (`NonceTooLowError`), or a replacement when the fee estimate is 10% higher. No double effect; the next attempt heals. WP-04 step 2 is the fix.
6. **Follow-up (item 50, understated by the backlog).** `RegistrySnapshot.rebuild` copies the grouped arrays per finalization (`outcomes.ts:159`, `:174`) and runs synchronously after every indexer run that stored a row. Measured with a probe on one key: an incremental refresh of one outcome takes 177 ms at 5,000 finalizations and 6.6 s at 20,000, during which no request is served. Two `push` lines fix the quadratic part; the memory growth is item 50 as written.
